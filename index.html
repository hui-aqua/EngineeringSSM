Six capability dimensions

Draft definitions and scoring guidance.

Geometry

Create, import, edit, and repair geometry, including curves, surfaces, solids, and geometric groups. This also considers how well geometry workflows connect to simulation.

Meshing

Generate and control computational meshes, including mesh types and sizing, local refinement, boundary groups, quality checks, and mesh import/export.

Material Model

Define material parameters and response behavior, including material libraries, linear and nonlinear constitutive laws, temperature or time dependencies, and assignment workflows.

Load Model

Define loads, boundary conditions, and environmental inputs, including spatial or time variation, load cases, constraints, and reusable input data.

Analysis Solver

Configure and run numerical analyses. Consider supported physics, static and dynamic analysis, nonlinear methods, coupling, and solver controls.

Results Analysis

Inspect, visualize, and quantify results, including fields and curves, filtering and comparison, animation, reporting, and data export.

Draft scoring guide (1–10)
1–2: Very limited; mostly manual or dependent on external tools. 3–4: Basic capability with significant workflow or scope limits. 5–6: Handles common baseline tasks. 7–8: Broad support for common workflows. 9–10: Mature, comprehensive, and well-integrated. Scores describe capability in each dimension, not overall product quality. Unassessed items are shown as “Not assessed.”
